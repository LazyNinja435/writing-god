# Scene 0001 — Medbay

The medbay's lights held that particular blue Sera had stopped noticing years ago—until a patient flinched under them. Marcus Hale did not flinch. He sat on the edge of the diagnostic bed with the posture of a man who had been summoned, not treated.

"You asked for a voluntary recall," Sera said. "Your words. Not the archive's."

Marcus rubbed his thumb along the seam of his coveralls. "I said the coolant inspection happened Tuesday. The log says Monday. One of us is wrong."

"One of *something* is wrong." Sera turned the terminal so he could see the hash string. "This certification timestamp is eighteen hours earlier than your log claims the event occurred. Uncorrupted files don't do that."

His laugh had no humor in it. "You're telling an engineer his memory's broken. That's a bold move, Doc."

"I'm telling you the record is broken. That's worse."

Silence stretched between the hum of the beds. Marcus looked at the floor, then at her—really looked, for the first time since he'd arrived.

"If I flag this," he said quietly, "they'll pull my clearance. Maybe yours."

"Then help me understand what you remember before I file anything."

He exhaled. "Tuesday. Ladder three. A smell like burnt sugar in the conduit." He stopped. "That's all you're getting without a lawyer."

Sera saved the hash comparison to her private queue—another small rebellion against procedure. "I'm opening a formal discrepancy investigation."

Marcus's jaw tightened, but he nodded once. "Then God help whoever thinks they can edit us and walk away clean."

When he left, the antiseptic smell felt thinner, as if the room had learned a new kind of doubt.
